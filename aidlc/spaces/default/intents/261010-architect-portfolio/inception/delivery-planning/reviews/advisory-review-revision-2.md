# Advisory Review — Delivery Planning Revision 2

This is an independent advisory review. The engine did not record it, because the Delivery Planning stage declares no reviewer. It informs Michael's decision at the gate and approves nothing.

**Verdict:** NOT-READY

Revision 2 mostly delivers what Michael asked for: an early slice, real Studio UI sooner, parallel batches, and grouped checkpoints. The plan's engine claims are largely accurate, and it states D1 to D5 honestly. But two things stop it being buildable as written. The first is how parallel work lands in `main` as per-Bolt pull requests. The second is that the CI gates every Bolt must pass do not exist until the last Construction stage. Several smaller gaps are listed below. All of them can be fixed inside Delivery Planning, and none reopens requirements, architecture or visual design.

## Assessment of the Five Requests

**1. Early end-to-end walking slice: mostly met.** U0 with S1 and S2 delivers create, save, preview and publish to a static page on local and fake dependencies. It keeps real boundaries (the strict C2 allow-list, an owner check, a sandboxed preview) and explicitly grants no deployment. The slice also matches the team's Walking Skeleton practice, which asks for the thinnest integrated path first. The plan's scope is optimistic for two Bolts (Finding 5), the missing CI and invariant tests weaken the guardrails (Findings 3, 5), and the preview rule is silently skipped (Finding 4).

**2. Real Studio UI earlier: met.** S2 gives a minimal real Studio page at CP1, and U11 (B27 to B29) moves to batch 3, roughly 20 Bolts earlier than before. That is consistent with the approved dependency graph, where U11 depends on U3, U7 and U1 and not on U9. The end-to-end journeys move to Build and Test, which is sensible. Integration with the real U9 is deferred to CP5 and stated as such.

**3. Parallel isolated work: partly met.** Swarm with stage-major is the right engine mechanism, and the plan correctly names the engine settings as an explicit owner decision (D2) and keeps one records owner. The ADR-012 atomicity argument (U3 and U9 in different batches) holds against `unit-of-work.md` constraint 7. But the mechanism by which Unit-level swarm merges become per-Bolt pull requests, and how the integration branch relates to `main` and to `org.md`, is undefined (Findings 1, 2). Until that is fixed, the "every change through a pull request" and "one commit per Bolt" practices are not demonstrably preserved.

**4. Group checkpoints without blanket approval: partly met.** Six checkpoints that list each Bolt with its evidence do reduce stops from about 30 to 6, and D3 and D5 are honest that nothing becomes a blanket approval. The individual decisions remain, and "exclude one Bolt" has no engine action (Finding 7). The plan identifies D2 to D5 but not the other gate-reduction options available (Finding 12), so the request to identify exactly which changes would help is only half answered.

**5. Preserve security review, preview verification, merge authorization, spending and deployment approval: met in intent, with gaps.** The Definition of Done keeps 🔒 review, 🧪 preview, individual approval, and a separate merge authorization (E10). Spending and deployment approvals (E2, E6, E8, E12) stay outside the plan. The gaps are that S1 and S2 skip preview without a stated exception (Finding 4), and that the CI enforcement behind these controls does not exist yet (Finding 3).

## Notes

- **Owner decisions D1 to D5 are necessary and clearly stated.** D1 is the only way to get a cross-unit slice under the engine's unit ordering, unless skeleton-on is chosen. It reopens Units Generation narrowly and touches no contract or architecture decision. D2, D3 and D4 are real practice or engine changes, and each says what happens if it is declined. D5 is a recommendation to answer the engine's question one way, and it correctly notes that autonomous mode would skip the checkpoints where merges are authorized. None of them invents blanket approval. D4 is understated (Finding 2) and D3 oversold (Finding 7).
- **Engine claims verified against the protocol files.** Accurate: stage-major plus swarm fans out per dependency batch; Plan Approval is asked as one grouped question per batch (each Unit still has its own receipt); a batch checkpoint follows each batch; an autonomous grant waives ordinary completion questions but not Plan Approval, verification command selection or failures; `set-construction-iteration` and `set-construction-execution` can change during Construction when the person's message is on record. Overstated or unverified: "only the first unit runs completely first" (skeleton-on rule) and mid-run switching after a skeleton-off unit-major U0 (Finding 11).
- **Checks that held up.** The batch grouping matches the approved dependency graph (U1 and U2; U3, U6 and U7; U4, U5, U8 and U11; U9; U10). B30 moves to Build and Test, so U11 no longer waits for U9. The Bolt count is 31 (S1, S2 and B01 to B29). Q1 to Q4 in the questions file were revised in place, with the original answers kept for the record.
- **Other things to weigh.** The pending records pull requests (#8 to #12) and E10 merge authorization decide when anything first reaches `main`. Preview (E2) must exist before any 🧪 Bolt merges, and almost every Bolt after the slice is 🧪. Until E2, approved work accumulates unmerged, which makes Finding 2 matter more.
- **Scope of this review.** I did not re-review requirements, architecture or visual-design decisions. Upstream artifacts were used only to check consistency (`unit-of-work.md` constraint 7 and `unit-of-work-dependency.md`).
