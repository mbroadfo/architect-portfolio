# Advisory Review — Delivery Planning Revision 3

This is an independent advisory review. The engine did not record it, because the Delivery Planning stage declares no reviewer. It informs Michael's decision at the gate and approves nothing.

**Verdict:** NOT-READY

Revision 3 is a clear improvement on revision 2. The engine claims I checked are accurate, D1 is necessary and minimal, and it drops the swarm landing problem that sank revision 2. Three problems remain, and each can be fixed inside Delivery Planning without reopening requirements, architecture or visual design:

- No Bolt builds the preview deployment path, so about 24 of the 33 Bolts cannot satisfy the plan's own "preview before merge" rule until after Construction.
- S1 to S3 are marked as needing preview verification, yet the plan says they never reach preview.
- The stacked-branch mechanism and the D2 with D3 approval model are weaker and less traceable than the plan says.

Paths below are relative to `aidlc/spaces/default/intents/261010-architect-portfolio/inception/delivery-planning/` unless they start with `aidlc/` or `.claude/`.

## Assessment of the Five Requests

**1. Early end-to-end walking slice: mostly met.** U0 and its four Bolts (S0 to S3) deliver create, save, preview and publish as a static page, with CI first, on local and fake dependencies. They keep the strict C2 allow-list, an owner check, a sandboxed preview, and the named tests for unauthenticated rejection, publish-only-on-request and no private material, plus a build-time check that the fake identity provider cannot ship. They grant no deployment. It satisfies the "thinnest integrated path first" practice. What is missing is the slice's own merge and verification status (finding 2) and the preview path that would let it, and everything after it, leave the stack (finding 1).

**2. Real Studio user interface earlier: met.** A minimal Studio page is in the slice (S3), and U11 moves from 11th to 6th through three ordering-only edges, to be built against the real U3 and U7 and contract fakes for U4, U5 and U9. B30 moves to Build and Test, so the Studio no longer waits for U9. The edges add no real dependency and do not conflict with the approved graph; the engine order is a stated assumption (finding 9).

**3. Revisit serial execution: partly met.** The plan evaluates swarm honestly and its engine claims are accurate: swarm lands one squash commit per unit, and `org.md` fixes the merge target as `main`. Staying unit-major and serial is a defensible choice, and it correctly says what swarm would need if Michael wants it later. The replacement, stacked per-Bolt branches, is not yet traceable or compatible with the short-lived-branch rule (finding 3). The hybrid path of independent agents on isolated branches that team practice already allows is not evaluated (finding 8). Exactly one owner of the AI-DLC state is preserved.

**4. Group checkpoints without inventing approval authority: partly met.** Six milestone reviews that list every Bolt individually with its evidence are a sensible grouping, and the engine-change list (autonomy, plan-approval guard, standing merge authorization, summary confirmation, records pull requests) is concrete and honest that nothing is assumed. But D2 plus D3 makes the engine auto-approve units and moves Michael's approval to a logged milestone answer, which is a weaker form than "Michael's own answer at an AI-DLC gate". The plan does not say so, and it leaves the `project.md` rule unamended (finding 4). The saving is about three stops (finding 5).

**5. Preserve security review, preview verification, merge authorization, and separate spending and deployment approval: met in intent, not yet in mechanism.** Every 🔒 Bolt still needs an independent review before approval, every merge still needs E10, and E1, E2, E6, E8 and E12 stay separate. But preview verification before merge has no mechanism: no Bolt builds the deploy path, and S1 to S3 cannot meet it (findings 1 and 2). The review record is bound to a commit that the stack rebases (finding 3).

## Notes

- **D1 to D3.** D1 is necessary: under unit-major the engine builds each unit completely and in turn, so a cross-unit slice can only come first as its own unit. It is minimal, with one unit, five ordering-only edges and the story-map rows, and it reopens no component, contract, boundary or ADR. Option C (`skeleton: on`) would also work, but it needs a team-practice change and adds a formal checkpoint, as the plan says. D2 is a legitimate use of the engine's own autonomy answer and is accurate about what it skips (completion questions) and what it keeps (Plan Approval, verification-command selection, failures). D3 is the weak point (finding 4). None of the three reopens architecture.
- **Engine claims verified.** Accurate: unit-major is serial and builds each unit completely, in dependency order; an autonomous grant waives ordinary completion questions but not Plan Approval, summary confirmation, verification-command selection or failures; the verification command is one intent-level, human-authorized command reused at every Unit checkpoint; swarm lands one squash commit per converged unit; `set-construction-iteration` and `set-construction-execution` are the setters. The engine does not consume `bolt-plan.md`, so Bolt-level branches and pull requests are the conductor's convention, not engine state. That is why the stack needs its own rules.
- **Scope of this review.** I did not re-review requirements, architecture or visual-design decisions. I used the dependency graph (`unit-of-work-dependency.md`) only to check that the proposed build order is a valid topological order after the new edges. It is, and no cycle is introduced.
