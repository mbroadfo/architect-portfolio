## Assessment of the Five Requests

**1. Early end-to-end walking slice: met in substance, with open points.** U0 gives a runnable create, save, preview and publish flow after four Bolts, with CI first, fakes locally, a build-time guard against the fake identity provider shipping, the C2 allow-list from the start, and no production path. That meets the request, and the 🔒 and 🧪 safeguards are kept. The slice does reimplement a thin publication boundary that U8 and U9 later own, and it is deliberately throwaway work: the plan lists five retirements but gives no cost for it. Its own approval and preview order is the gap (finding 2).

**2. Real Studio UI earlier: met.** S3 puts a minimal real page into the slice and U11 moves from 11th to 6th through three ordering-only edges, with the build order still a valid topological order and no cycle. The risk is what follows: U11 is approved before U4, U5 and U9 exist, and no Bolt owns the switch to real endpoints (finding 3). Edge semantics need documenting (finding 6).

**3. Serial execution and parallelism: well reasoned, though no decision is offered.** The engine claims I checked are accurate. Swarm lands one squash commit per converged unit and `org.md` fixes `main` as the target. The hybrid path with other harnesses is evaluated, and one owner of workflow state is kept. Staying unit-major and serial is defensible. The plan lists the changes swarm would need, but does not present them as an owner decision with the trade-off. Michael asked for an explicit proposal where an engine mode change needs one. A short D3-style line would satisfy that. The grouped Plan Approval the engine offers when several units are planned together is not mentioned, although it is the only engine lever that cuts the 12 Plan Approvals. It only works under stage-major or swarm.

**4. Group checkpoints without inventing approval authority: mostly met.** Making the engine's unit checkpoint the single meaningful stop is the right call, and the honest stop table (rising from 23 to 26 to 28) avoids overselling. D2-B and D2-C are correctly labelled as weaker and needing explicit amendments. What the plan still does not do is show how a per-Bolt decision is actually recorded and enforced inside a unit-level binary gate (finding 1), and whether merge is a decision of its own (finding 4). The list of practice and engine changes that would cut gates is concrete but short: D2-B, guard off and standing merge authorization. It omits grouped Plan Approval and the batching of records pull requests beyond one line.

**5. Preserve security review, preview before merge, merge authorization and separate spending approval: met in mechanism, with two gaps.** Every 🔒 Bolt still needs an independent review, bound to content. Every 🧪 Bolt now has a real path through B05a, and E1, E2, E6, E8 and E12 stay owner decisions. The gaps are the M1 approval of S1 to S3 before their preview evidence exists, and the preview inputs E3 and E4 that they need but are not tied to (finding 2).
